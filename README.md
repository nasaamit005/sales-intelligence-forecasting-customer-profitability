# Sales Intelligence, Forecasting & Customer Profitability Optimization Platform
**Live interactive dashboard: [nasaamit005.github.io/sales-intelligence-forecasting-customer-profitability](https://nasaamit005.github.io/sales-intelligence-forecasting-customer-profitability/)**

An end-to-end business analytics application: **data -> SQL -> analytics -> machine learning -> business
recommendations -> interactive dashboard -> automated reporting**, built on the Global Superstore sample
dataset (51,290 order lines, 2011-2014, 7 markets, 147 countries).

> **Data note.** The dataset is a *fictional* training sample. All results below describe that sample, not a real
> company. "Profit" is the source's reported field (not audited net profit). See `docs/DATA_SOURCE.md`.

## 1. Business questions and where they are answered

| Area | Question | Where |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue | How much is generated, and what drives growth? | Overview, Sales Analysis |
| Profitability | Which categories, products and markets earn the most reported profit? | Product / Regional Intelligence |
| Customers | Who is most valuable? Who may stop buying? | Customer Intelligence (RFM, cohorts, churn model) |
| Forecasting | What are expected sales over 30 / 60 / 90 days? | Forecasting, Demand Planning |
| Discounts | Do discounts earn their cost? | Pricing & Discounts |
| Geography | Which markets are strongest? | Regional Intelligence |
| Operations | Where might demand outrun capacity? | Demand Planning (scenarios only; see limits) |

## 2. Quick start


## 3. Architecture

```
data/raw  (read-only)  ->  src/salesintel/data.py        clean + validate  -> data/processed/
                       ->  database.py + database/*.sql   star schema in SQLite, SQL-vs-pandas reconciliation
                       ->  analytics.py                   KPIs, RFM, cohorts, historical value, churn model
                       ->  forecasting.py                 7 models, rolling-origin validation, held-out test
                       ->  commercial.py                  discount bands, opportunity matrix, demand scenarios
                       ->  reporting.py                   17-tab Excel workbook + HTML management report
web/ (Flask + Plotly, light theme, 9 pages, filters, CSV export)      scripts/run_pipeline.py  tests/
```

Star schema: `fact_sales`, `dim_date`, `dim_customer`, `dim_product`, `dim_region`, plus `fact_forecast`
(every validation/test forecast) and the view `v_monthly_kpis`. Raw data is never overwritten; every stage is
re-runnable (`test_cleaning_is_rerunnable`).

## 4. Data quality decisions (all measured on the real file)

* Dates arrive in two day-first formats; month-first parsing produces thousands of negative ship lags, day-first none.
* **659 order IDs are re-used** for different customers/dates, so an order = `order_id + date + customer_id`
  (25,754 orders, not 25,035).
* Customer *names* are not unique (795 shared), so `customer_id` is the customer key.
* No return/cancellation field exists; all lines are treated as completed sales (flagged in the data-quality report).
* SQL aggregates reconcile with pandas to 1e-6 for revenue, profit, orders, units and customers.

## 5. Results (generated by the pipeline; see `results/metrics.json`)

**Sales.** Revenue $12.64M, reported profit $1.47M (11.6% margin), 25,754 orders, 1,590 customers.
Revenue by year: $2.26M (2011), $2.68M, $3.41M, $4.30M (2014, +26.3%).

**Customers.** The top 20% of customers produce 46.7% of revenue. RFM "Champions" (379 customers) hold 45.2% of
revenue; "Hibernating" customers (433) hold 6.1% at the lowest margin (8.1%). Customer value shown is
*historical* revenue/profit to date, **not** a predicted CLV.

**Discounts (descriptive, not causal).**

| Discount band | Reported margin | Loss-making lines |
|---|---|---|
| 0% | 25.3% | 0% |
| 1-10% | 17.2% | 19.3% |
| 11-20% | 9.9% | 23.3% |
| 21-30% | -5.5% | 62.2% |
| 31-50% | -32.4% | 87.4% |
| >50% | -111.0% | 100% |

Lines discounted 31% or more are 12.2% of revenue but run at a -51.3% reported margin. 178 products with more than
$5,000 revenue earn under 2% margin. The **Tables** sub-category loses $64k on $757k revenue. Discount depth is not
randomly assigned, so confounding (for example by product mix, market or order size) is possible; these are
associations only and no promotion-uplift claim is made.

**Churn model** (logistic regression; "churn" = no purchase in the next 180 days; trained at the 2013-06-30 cutoff,
tested at 2014-06-30): **AUC 0.79** vs 0.68 for a recency-only baseline (1,553 / 1,588 customers; churn rate
19.6% train, 13.9% test). Frequency is the dominant signal.

### Forecasting

Weekly revenue (208 complete weeks, 2011-01-09 to 2014-12-28). **Selection** uses three expanding-window,
rolling-origin folds of 13 weeks (origins at weeks 156, 169, 182). The **last 13 weeks (Oct-Dec 2014) are a
held-out test block** that selection never sees (enforced by `test_selection_ignores_test_block`).

| Model | Validation WAPE (mean of 3 folds) | Held-out test WAPE |
|---|---|---|
| **XGBoost (YoY ratio)** (selected) | **17.2%** | 18.0% |
| XGBoost (levels) | 21.1% | 15.2% |
| ARIMA + Fourier seasonality | 22.8% | 18.3% |
| Seasonal naive + growth | 23.1% | 19.0% |
| Seasonal naive | 23.8% | 29.1% |
| Moving average (4w) | 38.7% | 15.2% |
| Naive | 39.8% | 15.2% |

**Read this honestly.** The selected model wins validation but is *not* best on the 13-week test block, where
naive and moving-average baselines score 15.2%. Thirteen weeks of Q4 is a short, noisy block, so neither result
settles which model is better; the practical conclusion is that forecast accuracy is about 15-20% WAPE at this
horizon and the baselines are hard to beat. Per-category results (same protocol) select different models
(Furniture: XGBoost levels; Office Supplies: XGBoost YoY; Technology: ARIMA), see
`results/category_forecast_evaluation.csv`.

**Next 13 weeks (from 2015-01-04):** base case **$886k**, approximate 80% interval $634k-$1,024k, which is **1.26x
the same weeks last year** (trailing 52-week growth: 26%). The interval is empirical (10th/90th percentile of
actual/forecast ratios across validation folds), not model-based.

#### A forecasting bug found and fixed during development
My first final forecast came out at **1.70x** the same weeks last year while trailing growth was 26%. Cause: an
XGBoost level model with a raw time-index feature locked onto the latest peak-season (Q4 2014) level and
overstated Q1 demand. Fixes: removed the time-index feature, added a growth-adjusted seasonal baseline, and added
the YoY-ratio model (anchors seasonality on last year's value, learns growth as a ratio). A permanent
regression test (`test_final_forecast_is_plausible_vs_last_year`) now guards this. **Disclosure:** the test-block
scores had already been printed when I made this change, so the test block is *no longer pristine* and its WAPE
should be read as indicative. The change was driven by the plausibility check, and it actually *worsened* the
test-block score of the selected model (12.3% -> 18.0%). ARIMA + Fourier still overshoots Q1 (1.5x); it is kept in
the comparison for transparency, and it is not selected.

## 6. Dashboard

Nine pages (Executive Overview, Sales, Product, Regional, Customer, Forecasting, Pricing & Discounts, Demand
Planning, Reports). Year / market / category / segment filters update every chart and KPI on the filterable pages;
every table has a filtered CSV export; Plotly charts export PNG; empty-filter and missing-report states are handled.
Charts are served from a local `plotly.min.js` (no CDN needed). Screenshots: `docs/screenshots/`.
The forecasting page is not filterable (forecasts are at total level).
**Two versions of the dashboard:**
- **Live static version** (`docs/index.html`, hosted on GitHub Pages): 7 tabs, with Year / Market / Category filters. It is rebuilt from the pipeline results by `scripts/build_static_dashboard.py` and loads Chart.js from a CDN, so it needs an internet connection.
- **Flask version** (`python app.py`): 9 pages, with CSV export and a local Plotly bundle, so it works offline.

## 7. Reports

* `reports/excel/sales_intelligence_report.xlsx`: 17 tabs (Executive Dashboard with native chart, KPIs, trends,
  products, categories, regions, RFM, cohorts, historical customer value, forecast comparison/accuracy, discounts,
  demand planning, data quality, data dictionary, catalog). Totals are verified against the pipeline in tests.
* `reports/html/management_report.html`: findings, forecast evaluation, limitations (print to PDF in a browser).
  **A PDF generator and a Power BI report were not built** (see the roadmap in section 10).

## 8. Testing

`pytest`: 51 tests, ~99% line coverage. They cover raw-file immutability, mixed-date parsing, SQL-vs-pandas
reconciliation, foreign keys, RFM reconciliation against raw transactions, feature leakage (lag, YoY and churn
features cannot see the future), chronological splits, selection independent of the test block, forecast
plausibility, Excel/HTML contents, and every dashboard route and filter. `ruff` passes.

## 9. Analytical limits (what this data cannot establish)

* **No causal discount claims**: observational data only.
* **No true profit**: profit is the reported field; shipping cost is shown separately and not subtracted.
* **No CLV forecast**: only historical value; the churn model predicts lapse, not value.
* **No inventory optimisation**: no stock/lead-time data, so Demand Planning gives unit-demand *scenarios*
  (units inferred from the trailing-52-week units-per-dollar ratio), not reorder points.
* Forecasts are total-level, trained on 4 years of weekly data; yearly seasonality rests on 3-4 cycles.
* Returns and cancellations cannot be identified.

## 10. Roadmap 

| Planned extension | Why it is not here yet |
|---|---|
| Power BI report | A second BI deliverable; the Excel workbook and dashboards cover reporting for now |
| Automated PDF report | The HTML management report can be printed to PDF from a browser |

| Additional datasets (UCI Online Retail II, Olist) | This version uses one dataset; they would add customer-forecast and delivery/seller analytics |
| Forward-looking CLV (BG/NBD + Gamma-Gamma) | Needs assumptions the current data does not support; only historical value is reported |

| MySQL / PostgreSQL | SQLite is used; the schema in `database/schema.sql` is portable |
